## Problem Statement

Aspiring data professionals — particularly students and juniors — face immense confusion and "job description fatigue" when entering the tech job market. Commercial job listings routinely demand exhaustive checklists of 10–20 technologies, while generic career roadmaps often offer uncalibrated skill lists without explaining what actually distinguishes one data discipline from another in practice.

Job seekers struggle to answer four fundamental questions:
1. **Role Differentiation:** What skills do employers actually look for in Data Analysts vs. Data Scientists vs. Data Engineers vs. ML/AI Engineers vs. AI Engineers?
2. **Foundational vs. Distinguishing Skills:** Which skills are universal "table stakes" across multiple roles, and which ones actually make a specific role distinct?
3. **Learning Prioritization:** If I want to pursue a specific career trajectory, which technical skills should I prioritize learning first?
4. **Geographic Variation:** How do technical requirements and hiring volumes vary across European countries?

This project addresses these questions by transforming an audited dataset of 24,488 European job postings into an interactive **evidence-based career exploration tool** that provides clear market insights without statistical jargon.

## What Makes This Cool

1. **The "Table Stakes vs. Role Superpowers" Insight:**
   Most career guides treat all skill mentions equally. This project introduces an intuitive two-dimensional model:
   - **Within-Category Prevalence ($P(\text{skill} \mid \text{role})$):** How common a skill is within a specific occupation (e.g., SQL is a foundational requirement across multiple data disciplines).
   - **Distinctiveness / Lift ($\text{Lift} = P(\text{skill} \mid \text{role}) / P(\text{skill} \mid \text{corpus})$):** How uniquely characteristic a skill is of a specific profession relative to the broader market baseline ($\text{Lift} = 1.00$).
2. **The Four-Quadrant Explanatory Framework:**
   Visualizing skills on an interactive 2D landscape (Within-Category Prevalence on the X-axis vs. Distinctiveness Lift on the Y-axis with an explicit baseline parity reference line at $\text{Lift} = 1.00$) provides an explanatory framework for interpreting the continuous bivariate space:
   - **Role Superpowers (Higher Prevalence, Lift > 1.00):** Frequently requested skills that are simultaneously disproportionately concentrated within the selected occupation (e.g., `Power BI` in Data Analytics, `Apache Spark` in Data Engineering, `PyTorch` in ML/AI).
   - **Universal Currency (Higher Prevalence, Lift ≤ 1.00):** Frequently requested technologies that appear across multiple data disciplines (such as `SQL`). They reflect broad market presence rather than occupational specialization.
   - **Niche Specializations (Lower Prevalence, Lift > 1.00):** Tools with lower overall posting volume within the category that nonetheless show strong occupational concentration relative to the rest of the corpus (e.g., `XGBoost` in Data Science, `OpenCV` in ML/AI).
   - **Peripheral Skills (Lower Prevalence, Lift ≤ 1.00):** Tools that appear infrequently within the selected occupation and at rates below or near the broader market baseline.
3. **Evidence-Based Exploration vs. Subjective Advice:**
   Rather than relying on subjective opinions or anecdotal advice, every insight is grounded in an audited dataset of 24,488 observed FreeHire job postings across all 27 EU member states, supported by 394 automated software tests and an audited skill-extraction pipeline.

## Concrete Audience Walkthrough: Interpreting Prevalence vs. Distinctiveness

To illustrate why prevalence and distinctiveness provide different but complementary information, consider how a junior data candidate evaluates **Python vs. Excel in Data Analytics**:

- **Python:** Appears in **42.2%** of observed Data Analytics postings (1,510 of 3,582 jobs). This sounds high at first glance. However, Python appears in 57.1% of the overall data corpus and in over 73% of Data Science and ML/AI postings. As a result, its distinctiveness Lift in Data Analytics is only **0.74×** (below the market baseline of 1.00). It is a valuable general-purpose tool, but it does not define the Data Analytics role.
- **Excel:** Appears in **22.1%** of Data Analytics postings (790 jobs). While its raw volume is roughly half that of Python, Excel has a Lift of **4.73×** — meaning an employer posting a Data Analytics role is nearly five times more likely to request Excel than the broader data job market.
- **The Junior's Takeaway:** A student looking at both metrics immediately realizes: *Python is a versatile foundation that keeps future doors open, but Excel and Power BI (44.8% prevalence, 2.61× lift) are the actual distinguishing tools that employers specifically seek when hiring a Data Analyst.*

## Constraints & Scope Boundaries

- **Strict Scope Boundaries:** Zero personalized CV matching, resume parsing, or automated skill-gap scoring algorithms (preserves documented project scope rules).
- **Comparative Functionality, Not Automated Advice:** Role-to-role comparisons are a storytelling framework enabled by the user switching between occupational filters in the dashboard. The application does not generate personalized career-transition recommendations or compute custom skill-gap counts.
- **Data Integrity:** All claims and visualizations reflect the audited snapshot of 24,488 unique EU-27 postings collected up to 7 November 2026.
- **Geographic Robustness:** Cross-country occupational composition charts are strictly restricted to countries with $N \ge 300$ observed postings (11 member states); smaller markets remain visible on the choropleth map with raw sample counts.
- **Testing Distinction:** Software tests (394 automated pytest tests verifying pipeline functions, atomic writes, thresholding, and UI rendering) are distinguished from manual dataset audits (e.g., the 50-posting manual validation sample and extraction precision audits).

We implement **Approach B** by authoring `docs/promo_page.md` structured into eight cohesive narrative sections, with all metrics verified directly against `data/processed/analytics_summary.json`:

1. **Hero Section:**
   - **Headline:** *The EU Data Career Compass: What the Job Market Actually Expects from You.*
   - **Subheadline:** *An interactive, evidence-based exploration of 24,488 tech postings across 27 European countries to help aspiring analysts, scientists, and engineers navigate real market demand.*
   - **Core Metric Badges:** 24,488 Postings | 27 EU Member States | 5 Occupational Categories | 52 Tracked Skills.
2. **The Problem: The Junior Data Dilemma:**
   - The trap of the 15-skill job description.
   - Why "just learn Python and SQL" is necessary but incomplete without understanding role specialization.
3. **The Core Framework: Popularity vs. Distinctiveness:**
   - Plain-English explanation of **Prevalence** ($P(\text{skill} \mid \text{category})$) vs. **Distinctiveness Lift** relative to the corpus baseline ($\text{Lift} = 1.00$).
   - The four standardized quadrants: **Universal Currency**, **Role Superpowers**, **Niche Specializations**, and **Peripheral Skills**.
   - The concrete Python vs. Excel in Data Analytics case study.
4. **The Five Data Professions Explored (Verified Data Callouts):**
   - **Data Engineering (13,592 postings, 55.5% of corpus):** The largest category. SQL (55.8%, 7,591 jobs) and Python (53.5%, 7,268 jobs) form the foundation, while Apache Kafka (1.61× lift, 11.5%), Scala (1.55× lift, 8.9%), Apache Spark (1.47× lift, 30.3%), and Apache Airflow (1.46× lift, 17.7%) act as role-defining infrastructure superpowers.
   - **Data Analytics (3,582 postings, 14.6% of corpus):** SQL leads in demand (63.7%, 2,283 jobs, 1.31× lift). The role is sharply differentiated by BI and spreadsheet tools: Power BI (44.8%, 1,603 jobs, 2.61× lift), Excel (22.1%, 790 jobs, 4.73× lift), Looker (11.5%, 3.90× lift), and Tableau (21.0%, 754 jobs, 3.38× lift). Python is present in 42.2% of postings but sits at 0.74× lift.
   - **AI Engineering (3,345 postings, 13.7% of corpus):** Dominated by modern GenAI stacks. LLMs / Generative AI leads overall demand at 81.8% (2,735 jobs, 3.27× lift). Key distinctive drivers include LangChain (20.9%, 698 jobs, 4.97× lift), TypeScript (11.9%, 4.69× lift), and Hugging Face (6.5%, 3.16× lift).
   - **Data Science (2,558 postings, 10.4% of corpus):** Python leads decisively at 73.8% (1,887 jobs, 1.29× lift), followed by SQL at 46.2% (1,182 jobs, 0.95× lift). The category is uniquely characterized by machine learning and statistical modeling tools: XGBoost (4.1%, 106 jobs, 5.51× lift), Scikit-learn (17.0%, 436 jobs, 4.33× lift), NumPy (9.1%, 4.02× lift), R (16.4%, 420 jobs, 3.43× lift), and Pandas (14.1%, 3.34× lift).
   - **ML/AI (1,411 postings, 5.8% of corpus):** High technical intensity. Python is requested in 74.4% (1,050 jobs, 1.30× lift). Deep learning frameworks and vision tools dominate distinctiveness: OpenCV (2.8%, 9.02× lift), C++ (12.7%, 6.74× lift), PyTorch (40.0%, 565 jobs, 6.70× lift), TensorFlow (25.2%, 5.74× lift), and MLflow (15.5%, 5.65× lift).
5. **Interactive Experience Walkthrough:**
   - Step 1: Global Occupation Selection (drill into any of the 5 roles).
   - Step 2: Skill Family Filter (isolate Programming, Cloud, BI, Databases, or ML/AI).
   - Step 3: Quadrant Scatter Plot (hover to inspect exact volume, prevalence, and lift multiplier).
   - Step 4: Geographic Benchmark (explore how country composition varies across Europe).
6. **The Geographic Reality Check:**
   - 27 EU member states mapped on the choropleth view.
   - Occupational composition comparison restricted to the **11 countries with $N \ge 300$ postings**: France (4,972), Netherlands (3,307), Poland (2,826), Spain (2,740), Germany (2,396), Italy (2,129), Portugal (1,366), Belgium (1,012), Sweden (885), Ireland (470), and Denmark (416).
7. **Under the Hood (Engineering & Pipeline Integrity):**
   - Transparent methodology: hybrid skill extraction (native platform tags prioritized, delimiter-guarded regex fallback), controlled 52-skill canonical vocabulary, atomic JSON caching.
   - Robust quality assurance: 394 automated software tests and documented empirical validation holdouts.
   - Descriptive seniority reporting: transparently noting that seniority metadata is available for 42.9% of postings (10,494 of 24,488 postings) without artificial imputation.
8. **Call to Action & Student Takeaway:**
   - Dashboard launch command (`uv run streamlit run app.py`).
   - The concrete takeaway: leave with clarity on what to learn next based on real market evidence.
